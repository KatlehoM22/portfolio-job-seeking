# Project overview

The iHub Attendance Portal is a deployed internal web application for trainees,
managers, and administrators. It connects attendance data from DreamClass with
the day-to-day workflows needed to manage attendance across the organisation.

Instead of relying on disconnected records, manual request processes, and
separately prepared reports, the portal gives each user a workspace tailored to
their responsibilities.

- **Status:** Deployed internal application
- **Integrations:** DreamClass and configurable email services

## The problem

DreamClass provided the source attendance data, but it did not cover all the
operational processes surrounding attendance management. The organisation
needed a central place where:

- trainees could understand their attendance and submit absence, late-arrival,
  or early-departure requests;
- managers could resolve requests and identify attendance risks;
- administrators could access programme-wide reporting and system controls;
- attendance statuses, policies, and reporting rules could remain consistent;
- integrations and important system activity could be monitored.

Without a centralised workflow, attendance information and operational
decisions were difficult to track and time-consuming to report on.

## My role

- developing role-specific dashboards for trainees, managers, and

## The solution

### Role-based workspaces

**Trainees** can review attendance rates and term summaries, inspect their
history, submit attendance-related requests, track decisions, and receive
notifications.

**Managers** can monitor assigned trainees, resolve requests, review trends and
strike alerts, inspect province-wide attendance history, and generate scoped
reports.

**Administrators** can access organisation-wide analytics, manage users and
assignments, configure attendance rules and programme phases, export data,
review audit and synchronisation activity, and monitor database, email, and
DreamClass service health.

### Request lifecycle

The portal supports absence or leave, late-arrival, and early-departure
requests. The backend validates each submission against configurable timing and
leave policies before storing it.

Requests move through explicit states: **pending**, **approved**, **rejected**,
**cancelled**, or **rescinded**. Managers are notified when action is required;
trainees receive the outcome; and every decision remains available in the audit
history.

### DreamClass integration

DreamClass remains the upstream source for trainee and attendance data. Through
scheduled and manual synchronisation, the portal:

- retrieves trainee and attendance data;
- maps DreamClass users to portal users;
- normalises inconsistent attendance statuses;
- stores validated records in PostgreSQL;
- calculates dashboard metrics from local data;
- records synchronisation results for administrators.

Keeping a normalised local copy improves reporting performance and avoids
depending on the external API for every dashboard request.

## Key engineering decisions

### Backend-enforced data scoping

Permissions are enforced by the API, not only by hiding interface elements.
Trainees can access only their own records, while manager access is determined
by assignment, province, and the feature being used.

A manager may view attendance history for all trainees in their province, but
request management and other sensitive actions remain assignment-based. This
feature-specific rule provides the required visibility without widening access
across the rest of the system.

### Local attendance storage

Serving reports directly from DreamClass would couple availability and
performance to an external API. Normalised PostgreSQL records provide faster
queries, historical reporting, consistent statuses, stronger data integrity,
and access to previously synchronised data during an upstream interruption.

### Configurable business rules

Programme start times, request cut-off times, leave allowances, attendance
statuses, and programme phases can change. I stored these as application
settings instead of repeating them as hard-coded logic.

### Operational visibility

Migrations, schema checks, audit records, synchronisation logs, scheduled jobs,
and provider-aware health checks help administrators distinguish between
database, DreamClass, email-provider, and application failures.

## Technical challenges

### Normalising external statuses

DreamClass could return inconsistent values for the same attendance concept. I
created central normalisation logic so calculations, charts, filters, badges,
and reports share one internal vocabulary.

### Managing date-based policies

Requests can span multiple dates and must be evaluated against programme
phases, reporting periods, and leave policies. Explicit date-range and overlap
logic keeps validation and reporting behaviour consistent.

### Protecting relational integrity during synchronisation

Every attendance record must belong to a recognised trainee. The synchronisation
process establishes trainee records before inserting attendance data, while
PostgreSQL foreign-key constraints prevent orphaned records.

## Outcome

The portal brings attendance records, trainee requests, manager decisions,
reporting, and administrative governance into one operational environment.
Trainees gain clearer visibility into their attendance, managers can focus on
exceptions and pending actions, and administrators receive a programme-wide
view of attendance operations and system health.

Technically, the project demonstrates end-to-end delivery across third-party
integration, multi-role workflows, backend authorisation, relational data
modelling, configurable rules, analytics, reporting, auditability, and system
monitoring.

## What I learned

- Access control must cover the full request lifecycle, not only the interface.
- External integrations need normalisation, logging, and clear failure states.
- Operational dashboards are most useful when they surface actions and
  exceptions, not only charts.
- Policies that are expected to change should be configurable.
- Internal applications need migrations, monitoring, and recovery processes
  alongside user-facing features.

## Future improvements
